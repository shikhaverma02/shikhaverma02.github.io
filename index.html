Minimal Coffee Table

Less is more

In a world full of noise and clutter, there is a growing appreciation for simplicity. A simple centre coffee table can be a complete statement of style, the one piece a living room needs.

The table is inspired by minimalism, which is a way of living as much as a design trend: living with less, focusing on what matters and finding beauty in simplicity. Two half-moon marble legs with circular cut-outs cross beneath a round top.

Industry briefDesign a marble centre coffee table. It should be simple and production-friendly.
Studio
Rayman Artefacts Pvt. Ltd.
Material
Marble, in a matte or honed finish
Material options
  • Classic Travertine
  • Titanium Travertine
  • Calacatta Viola
  • Golden Portoro
Assembly

Flat-packs into three parts (top, bridge plate and legs) and assembles in about 15 minutes with a screwdriver.

  1. Set the legs in a cross.
  2. Screw the legs to the bridge plate.
  3. Lay the top on the bridge plate and lock it in.

Loto Coffee Table

Inspired by the lotus leaf

The lotus grows in deep mud, far from the sun, and still reaches the light to become the most beautiful flower. Life works the same way: we work hard all day, and one cup of coffee refreshes the mind. That idea became this coffee table.

Loto means “lotus” in Italian. The top is radially inlaid in dark and light oak veneer and sits on a light antique brass base. At its centre is a CNC-carved metallic lotus leaf, taken from the leaf’s pattern and texture. The leaf stands for purity, resilience and serenity, and the table brings that calm into everyday use.

The table is fully knock-down. The top, bridge plate and base fit together with screws and an Allen key. It went from drawing to workshop to a finished, photographed piece.

Industry briefDesign a centre coffee table. It should be simple and production-friendly.

Rayman Artefacts Pvt. Ltd.

  • Light antique brass
Detail

.03

Salita Credenza

Arches in brass

A metal credenza works in a living room, dining room, office or bedroom, as storage and as a statement piece. Metal finishes range from brushed steel and matte black to bold colours, so the piece can blend in or become the focal point of a room.

The shutters are built in two layers of MDF. An 11 mm fluted layer is set into pockets routed in a 17 mm base. They are then clad in metal and finished in dark antique with a lacquer coat. The arches come from the mood board’s concentric, rainbow-like geometry.

Industry briefDesign a storage furniture piece in metal. It should be simple and production-friendly.
Studio
Rayman Artefacts Pvt. Ltd.

  • Dark antique brass

Bilancia Console

Sacred geometry

Sacred geometry blends mathematics, art, spirituality and science. It studies the shapes, patterns and proportions that carry symbolic meaning across cultures, often believed to connect with the basic principles of the universe.

This console turns those proportions into furniture. Stone petals are framed in brass beneath a slim top, so the piece works as both art and function. The petals can be cut from onyx, alabaster or green aventurine to change the console’s mood.

CNC Carving & Wall Murals

From ArtCAM to the machine bed

ArtCAM is used to model 2D and 3D relief surfaces and to generate the CNC machining program. Each panel is designed on screen and taken through to the machined piece, then built into furniture.

Intaglio Console (intaglio is Italian for carving) combines marble and MDF and is planned around CNC-carved panels. Bloom Cabinet uses brass-clad, flower-shaped CNC panels as its handles, which gives the piece a distinctive look.

The 3D wall murals are carved in relief on marble surfaces. At World of Stones I also designed wall panels for architects and interior designers, and peacock murals for Jodhpur airport.

.07

Vertical Garden Solution

Academic project · symmetry in nature

A vertical garden for middle- and upper-middle-class families in cities, who want a garden at home but don’t have the space. Growing upward makes a garden possible in a small footprint. It also cools the surroundings and freshens the air, which matters where pollution is a daily problem.

The form follows nature’s law of “equity”, the balance and symmetry that guide growth along the lines of greatest structural efficiency. Triangular planters link into modules that can be arranged in many configurations.

BriefDesign a range of products for a specific area. I chose a vertical garden for outdoor spaces in urban homes, inspired by symmetry in nature.
Context
National Institute of Fashion Technology
Process
Brainstorming → inspiration → form generation → final sketches → prototyping
Audience
Urban families with limited outdoor space
